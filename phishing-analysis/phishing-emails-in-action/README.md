# 📧 Phishing Emails in Action

## 📌 Overview

This TryHackMe room provided practical exposure to identifying phishing
attempts by examining realistic phishing emails.

The room focused on recognizing common phishing indicators and
understanding how attackers use social engineering to manipulate
victims.

## 🎯 Objectives

- Identify common phishing indicators
- Analyze suspicious email content
- Recognize social engineering techniques
- Identify suspicious links and attachments
- Understand common phishing scenarios

## 🧠 What I Learned

I learned how attackers use social engineering techniques to make
malicious emails appear legitimate and encourage victims to take
specific actions.

Common indicators of phishing include:

- Urgent or threatening language
- Suspicious links
- Unexpected attachments
- Requests for sensitive information
- Fake account notifications
- Fake delivery or payment notifications
- Impersonation of trusted organizations

## 🔎 Phishing Investigation

When analyzing a suspicious email, I learned to consider:

1. Who sent the email?
2. Does the sender's domain appear legitimate?
3. Are there suspicious links?
4. Are there unexpected attachments?
5. Is the message creating unnecessary urgency?
6. Is the recipient being asked to provide sensitive information?
7. Are there other indicators suggesting malicious activity?

## 🔐 SOC Analyst Relevance

Phishing emails can be used as an initial access technique by
attackers.

A SOC Analyst may need to investigate suspicious emails, identify
Indicators of Compromise (IOCs), determine the potential impact, and
support incident response activities.

This room helped me better understand how phishing analysis contributes
to threat detection and incident response.

## 💡 Key Takeaway

Phishing attacks often rely heavily on human psychology. Attackers
use urgency, fear, curiosity, authority, and trust to convince victims
to interact with malicious content.

Understanding these techniques helps security analysts identify
suspicious emails more effectively.
